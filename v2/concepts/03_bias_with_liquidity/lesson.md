# Bias and liquidity working together

Taught by Sharif on 18 Nov 2026 in a learn session, on the Russell 2000, walking one morning down from the monthly to
the 5-minute. He said the lesson was done the same day. It builds on `01_bias` and `02_liquidity` and adds no new
kind of mark except the trading range and its 50%.

**Shown back on 19 Nov 2026 on two charts I had not seen** (gold and the Nasdaq). He: "the read so far has been pretty
good", "so far not so bad", "nice catch", with the corrections below under "The bigger trade". He said that part
was done the same day. **Confirmed on 20 Nov 2026** on a third chart I had not seen (the Dow, read top to bottom in the
London morning): "so far so good, your read is solid."

## Know the bias first (his emphasis when closing the lesson)

- "Put emphasis on knowing what the bias is, and understanding it's okay to trade against a bias, but knowing what
  you're doing."
- "If we are with the bias we can have runner TPs or look for other liquidity to reach; if we aren't with the bias,
  grab what's quick and small."
- "Notice and remember how this buy is against the current bias… so if we buy from where we are, our TPs must be
  nearer points."

So before anything else: what is the bias on the timeframe above the one being traded, and is this idea with it or
against it. That answer sets how far the targets go.

## Two timeframes can disagree, and neither is wrong

- "The two are conflicting and that's natural… it's important to understand neither is incorrect."
- "The same can apply to monthly and weekly, or weekly and daily, or daily and 4 hours; it's the same thinking."
- **Favouring.** "This is where favouring occurs": if he sees a closed candle beyond the higher timeframe's area,
  against it, he favours the lower timeframe's side. If the area "keeps candles above it and it's still not letting
  candles break below, this tells me this BISI is validated. Validated just means it's proved its value, it's worth
  something."
- "Within that area I'd be looking to see something play out; whatever plays out will determine whether we are
  looking towards buyside or sellside."
- **Let price tip its hat.** "Remember to let price tip its hat to you… I would have to see movement or something
  play within that area to give me clear context on whether this move is possible."

## What "something playing out" looks like (the sequence he walked me through)

1. "First thing you'd have to notice is anything of importance, such as liquidity marked out."
2. "Notice we are in the area we found interesting; price took out a form of liq… then after it did that it had
   displacement… that same displacement left a FVG."
3. "Notice how the displacement also had a BOS."
4. "Once we see this, we would start looking at the trading range."
5. Then the 50% of that range.

**Naming, his preference:** "FVG I'm looking to enter, BISI I'm looking to reach; that's why I like BISI / SIBI on
higher timeframes." Settled when he closed the lesson: "FVGs for entries, SIBI / BISI for alt-liq."

**Displacement, in his words:** "almost like a quick rapid movement of price, and it sweeps some form of liquidity,
creates a BOS and leaves us some value (OB, breakers, FVGs). For now just FVGs is enough to work on." Order blocks
and breakers are not in v2 yet.

How it is seen: "notice how price is moving at a specific pace, it's almost a steady pace, and suddenly it just
shoots aggressively. It doesn't mean anything if it's not in an area of interest or a place we are watching."

Where it is looked for (his note, 20 Nov 2026): "For displacement you're looking for it to happen within our key area,
our interesting area. So, example: we have a SIBI or BISI, and we are expecting to sell from there maybe, or buy into
it, but it depends on where price is right now. So let's say it's almost at our SIBI: buying into it isn't so wise,
it's almost already there. We would be expecting a tipping hat within that SIBI or around that zone. And remember the
sessions, that's important too: a displacement around those times too are nice."

## The trading range

- It runs from the low the move began from to the high where the first pullback began (for a move up), wick to wick.
  The wording is mine; he corrected my marks twice (first too far, to a later high; then too short, at the
  displacement candle's high), I gave this reason, and he said "yes, that's correct".
- "If we are looking to go up, the bottom portion is discounted and the upper portion is expensive. If we are looking
  to go down, the top portion is expensive, the bottom portion is discounted. When we are selling we are looking to
  sell something expensive; when we are buying we are looking to buy it discounted."
- Price reaching the lower half when looking up: "that's a good area to buy from."
- **It does not have to get there.** "Imagine… price failed to reach the 50% of the trade range and it didn't break
  into the lower portion, but we are looking to go up: even if it didn't hit those zones it's still possible for
  price to go up. We might have just missed it."

## The bigger trade (taught 19 Nov 2026, on the show-back)

What he was teaching, in his words: "understanding when drawing bias, liq and trading ranges that you might have
missed the trade, because I kept seeing you draw ranges and areas that were very relative to now's trade; and that's
good, but remember there's a bigger trade. That bigger trade has importance, and it can assist in understanding the
smaller piece you're looking for within."

- **Top to bottom, always.** "Start on monthly, remember to do that, and go down; no matter what, it's top to bottom."
- **Ask whether it already passed.** "You're constantly looking for current areas for the current trade and current
  trading range… have you ever considered you missed it? Meaning it already passed." When the higher timeframe's
  range has already been sold from its expensive half (or bought from its discounted half), what is left lower down
  is the continuation: "we break lower timeframes, because now… we see the momentum… it's already moving, the entry
  was missed."
- **When a trading range is drawn.** "The trading range should be drawn when you're looking for a trade."
  "Normally try to show what's relevant to now."
- **The range is fluid.** "You should allow for the trading range to move, a bit more fluid, because expect a
  retracement… even if a low was broken below with a body, the trading range can expand."
  - "A break on the low can happen and it can have a closed body candle break, but it can't be too deep if we expect
    a retracement into our FVG."
  - Whether the top steps down to a retracement high once the low after it is broken: "technically yes, and only
    yes if the retracement that happened was deep enough." How deep: "it's tricky to say, but for now say 50%, but
    mark that it's tricky to say." **This is a working answer, not a settled rule.**
  - What decides which high or low ends a range: "TF is important, but also respecting higher TF highs or lows is
    important; they are more significant than smaller TF ones." A bounce that only shows on the smaller timeframes
    does not move the top of a higher timeframe's range, even when it comes back past the middle. (The last sentence
    is mine: it is how I read his drawing against mine. He answered my question with the quote.)
- **An area keeps the power of its own timeframe.** "Current TF and your important areas' TF are not the same level
  of power." A closed lower-timeframe candle beyond a higher-timeframe imbalance "doesn't invalidate it"; "we would
  need to see it close" on the area's own timeframe. The check he showed: on the area's own timeframe "it's just
  wicks" in it, which "also tells us price really pushed it down".
- **The 50% against the imbalance, as the entry.** "Sometimes respecting the 50% and your better pricing is an
  advantage, but sometimes it's a loss." Price can wick into the imbalance and leave before reaching the 50%; another
  time it comes all the way. "This doesn't always happen: sometimes you'll get the better bang for your buck… or you
  don't."
- **Know what was swept, even though it is off the chart.** Of equal highs already eaten: "I know we normally don't
  chart it, but you would have to know it was a sweep, because this was a time to get interesting price action."
  When that happens inside the area being watched, at the 50% of the range: "from here we should have been eyes
  open."
- **The first target with the bias.** He would have "stretched TP 1 a bit", past the nearest resting low to the far
  edge of an imbalance below it, "only because I'm expecting price to sell, the bias states so". Nearest-only is for
  an idea against the bias.

### His answers to my questions (19 Nov 2026)
- **"Deep enough":** "To make it simple and give you a number, just say 50%, because it's tricky to show to a
  machine. A consolidation could be happening for a while under that area, just below the 50%, and it might drop a bit
  lower, but it should still have, and be fluid with, its trading range… we will try work on it over time; as I explain
  things, try catch what I fix." Still a working number, not a settled rule.
- **How far the targets go:** "Relative to the trade range, what are you looking for: are you trading the big piece
  itself, to the end, or are you trading a smaller piece of a bigger piece? And you must know that. TF is important:
  if you're seeing a BISI and we are inside it at daily, the swing to get to your TP might take some time, and longer
  than what you'd expect… It goes back to looking for nearby liq, and if you want to allow a runner, allow it; but
  does bias allow it, and what liq is a runner going for?"
- **A sweep against equal highs:** I asked whether a high slightly past equal highs joins the set unless it wicks over
  and closes back under. "Yes, but a sweep is almost clearly not an EQH: the highs are nearby but it was clearly an
  'eat this high' situation. EQHs and EQLs are quite close and are areas price might want to eat."
- **Small equal highs inside the area being watched count as context.** They "played a part, and it wouldn't be wise
  to not acknowledge it; they more support what we were trying to do, which is find context within the current
  special area."
- **Trailing a runner** (from his answer on sessions): "with runners try to have a trailing stop; don't trail for
  TP 1, you'll normally get knocked out early if you do so; and don't trail until the trade range you're currently
  focusing on is hit."

- **Going a timeframe deeper for the entry** (my question, shown to him on the chart he first taught on): "Going in
  deeper is good, and I think that's your call; it does offer better entries, and better RRs, but you could get
  knocked out, but it is a good way to think."
- **An area price never reached is a miss, like the 50%.** On that same chart price turned before the resting
  liquidity and the higher-timeframe imbalance above it: "the same logic of a trading range failing to meet the 50%
  at least, because if we were looking to sell and wanted price to hit that for a sell, it didn't reach."

### My mistakes on the show-back, and what he said
- I drew a trading range while not looking for a trade. He: "you shouldn't have drawn the trading range."
- I kept the top of a range at the first high and only moved the bottom, making one huge range. He drew the ranges
  leg by leg.
- I then stepped a top down to a small bounce that had passed the middle. He kept the top at the higher timeframe's
  high.
- I read only the newest leg as "the" range and never drew one on the higher timeframe. He: "this one is tricky, the
  trading range is what I just marked… it's another one you missed."
- I let a high that went slightly past equal highs join the set, and wrote that I could not tell whether liquidity
  had been taken. It had been swept.
- I jumped from the bias table straight to the lowest timeframe.

### A rhythm he asked me to learn by watching (not a rule, his words)
"You might have to learn it on your own." Inside the New York morning: an early move, then the move playing out,
then "we normally missed it and are catching the continuation". Some days the early stretch is price driving into an
important area and the turn from it comes later. "Don't be fixed to this, just be fluid enough to understand this
key point… when we do backtesting I'll also need you to notice this pattern." The clock times he gave for it are in
`04_sessions`; they are approximate by his own word.

## The trade, on the timeframe being traded

- "We would be looking to buy in this FVG. This is where you would have to decide what timeframe you're currently
  trading on."
- **Entry:** "a buy limit at either the high of that FVG, or middle, or low." Which one "depends on what you're
  willing to risk; they can help improve return ratios."
- **Stop:** on the sellside the move left from, exactly on it, "because if it sweeps it we are discouraged."
- **Targets:** "the trading range high and the buyside" of that same timeframe.
- For a sell it is the mirror. That sentence is mine: he taught it on a buy, and gave both directions only for the
  halves of the range.

## Runners and partials (his answers when closing the lesson)

- "The most important part is knowing if we can have a runner or not." A runner "is left with bias": only when the
  idea is with the bias.
- "We plan to take off partials from our trade as TPs hit. TP 1 is always 80% of the trade."
- The rest is split over the liquidity left to reach, and the split is the trader's call: "you can do an even split
  or a favoured split." Even: with two further targets, the remaining 20% is split evenly between them. "Or if you
  feel it should be different it's okay."
- **Against the bias:** the "whole trade comes off at TP 1, but if there is very close-by liq you can leave the 20%
  for that small piece; it's your call to decide." "Grab what's close by and not too far off, what's nearby."
  - His example, an illustration only: on the trade he was showing, his second target would have been an old high
    sitting just past the first target. A gap further up was also "some form of liq", and "I wouldn't try reach" it.

## The timeframe is the key

- "Timeframes are fluid, so based on what timeframe I'm trading is key."
- The same sweep and the same displacement give a different entry on each timeframe. "If we were using [a lower
  timeframe] we might have seen a different approach and an earlier approach."
- "All this is building on the lower timeframe": when price came back to the 50% on the timeframe he was showing, the
  same sequence (liquidity taken, displacement with a BOS, a FVG left) built again one or two timeframes down. He:
  "very important, that's where timeframe is very important."
- Targets come from the timeframe being traded. Asked where to aim from a lower-timeframe FVG: "keep the tf in mind."
  The answer he accepted was that timeframe's own range high and its own nearest liquidity, not the higher
  timeframe's.

## Higher-timeframe liquidity as a target

He asked whether it would be wise to aim for the higher timeframe's liquidity above. The answer he accepted
("perfect, you got what I want"): not while the timeframe above is against the idea and an area of its own sits in
the way; that area "is an interesting area for price to take lower prices".

## What he corrected (kept on purpose)

- **I called a pullback shallow when it had reached the lower half.** He: "No, you got it wrong: the fact that price
  reached the lower portion of the trade range, which is the lower 50%, that's a good area to buy from."
- **I ended a read with a condition about the future** ("it would become wise to aim higher once…"). He: "cut this
  off… that's expecting price to do what we want; we would have to see it happen already."
- **I marked the trading range wrong twice** (see above).
- **I marked two neighbouring lows as equal lows on the timeframe they sat on.** He pointed back to the liquidity
  lesson: they are equal lows only on a lower timeframe.

## Left to the reading, on purpose (no tool yet)

The trading range as a tool is held for later by him (20 Nov 2026): "trading range, consider it later; it's tricky, but
keep the fluidity in your mind." Until then it is read by eye, and it stays fluid: it moves and expands.

No tool was written for this lesson. Finding displacement and the leg that makes a trading range has not been taught
as something to pick out mechanically, so it is read on the chart for now. The chart can draw a trading range and its
50% by hand.
