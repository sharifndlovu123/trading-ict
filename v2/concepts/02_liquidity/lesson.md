# Liquidity (in progress: being taught, not yet confirmed as a whole)

Started 17 Nov 2026 on the DAX (weekly, daily, hourly), from the cleaned references. No eyes code yet.

## From the references
ICT (rulings §12, §18; 2022 mentorship; core content):
- Buy stops rest above old highs (buyside), sell stops below old lows (sellside). "Price only respects where the actual liquidity is."
- Equal or smooth highs / lows are the larger pool: "a larger pool of liquidity… a stronger draw."
- The market builds liquidity, then takes it. "We don't rush in there and just go short because it went above old highs. We're looking for some specific signature."
- After external liquidity is swept, expect internal. After a pool is reached, expect a retracement or consolidation.

Sharif (his own words, quoted in the same notes; "my references should be accounted for, they are clear and concise as well as ICT's own"):
- Liquidity = trendlines, equal highs / lows, single levels (previous day / week highs and lows).
- Single levels line up with a nearby equal pool; once the pool is eaten price follows on to the single level.
- Pools are where others take profit, so target them.
- "The market will always create and kill liquidity."

## Taught on the chart, 17 Nov 2026 (his words)
- **"Draw it based on what you see right now."** / **"Show me liquidity relative to now."** Liquidity is marked for where price is now. A level that was liquidity for an earlier price (a low that was run days ago) is not liquidity now, even if today's candles cross it again. (My mistake: I marked the 5 November low as "eaten today"; it had been run the next day. He: "that's valid, but it's valid for when price was at that price.")
- **"Start from weekly, I know it's far up but it's important, that's another hint to bias, but remember relative to where we are now."** Read it top-down: the weekly pools first, then daily, then lower. What was just taken and what is left close by says something about the bias.
- On thinking of liquidity on its own or this way: his suggestion is this way (top-down, beside the bias, relative to now). My answer, which he did not reject: one simple definition of what it is; which liquidity matters is read top-down.
