## The key (his words, 17 Nov 2026)
**"Context is important, and where that context is, and time is key."** Every observation gets three tags before it counts: **what** happened (liquidity eaten or created, a displacement, a body closing in a gap), **where** (inside or against which higher-timeframe area), **when** (which session and window; before or after the first strong displacement). The same event somewhere else, or in a dead hour, means less.

## Every look, in this order
1. **Conditions.** Is the market moving cleanly and fast enough to even consider a trade? Clean = follow-through one way, shallow pullbacks, the three indices together. Choppy = large candles both ways in one range, pushes taken back, the indices split. **Big candles are not good conditions** if they go nowhere. Chop can still lean one way. A truly consolidative day: the arrays do not hold, they are broken through both ways (silver, 17 Nov). In chop: read and learn, say "not a market to trade". **What ends the chop is the first strong displacement**, and it is often the one that opens the entry (17 Nov: chop 07:00–09:20, displacement 09:30). Prefer the index that made it.
2. **Eaten or created.** What liquidity did the market just take, and what did it leave? "Market will either create liq or eat liq." **Both can come before the same move:** it can eat the highs and sell, or leave the highs and sell. The sweep is never a condition. **When it is creating, look for displacement on the opposite side:** liquidity left → a displacement away from it that breaks structure → the return to the array that displacement leaves is the entry (US100 03:30).
3. **The trade and its goal.** Name them and keep them in view: **the trade** (which side, and which higher-timeframe array said so), **the goal** (the level the higher timeframe names, e.g. the London low), **what is still missing** (usually a clean entry: then wait, be patient). His first thing for me to fix: understand the higher timeframe against the lower, and do not lose the trade for a small range inside it. A later, smaller entry to the same goal is the same trade.
4. **Where is price right now.** "Never try to force price to do what you want; you always check where it is now." Which arrays is it respecting?
5. **What, where, when** for anything new (the key above).

## Before the session (an hour before London 02:00 NY and New York 09:30 NY)
- Clean chart, hunt badge on (`hunt` in the overlay), watcher on; one-shot timers for the watch-time limits ([[hunting-process]]). Nothing is drawn outside a hunt. Never the Asian session. Gold and silver are for looking only.
- **Top-down from the monthly.** Long-term and short-term stated separately. Every idea is with or against; scenarios stay open. The monthly view can stay valid while price shows the opposite right now: trade what price is showing.
- **Higher-timeframe gaps and blocks, old ones included.** A gap ahead is first a target (its near edge), then a question.
- **Liquidity at every high and low,** and especially **built against the edge of an area** (equal highs under a bearish gap).
- **Compare the three:** weakest / strongest, cleanest pool, divergence (= SMT), and **which one is moving cleanly**. Take the trade on the cleaner one ("US500 ate the top and bottom, it looks messy").

## Reading an area
- **Bodies tell the story, wicks less.** A gap with only wicks into it is respected ("no body closing upwards into it, that's nice").
- **Match the timeframes.** A 4h area is judged on 4h candles; equal highs made on the 1h on 1h closes. Name the timeframe whenever I say respected, accepted, closed inside.
- **Be lenient going down.** A 4h gap retested by 4h wicks will have lower-timeframe bodies inside it; that does not invalidate it: "smaller pieces of a bigger puzzle".
- **A same-timeframe close inside a block weakens it** (not broken; expect less). Lower-timeframe closes inside are normal.
- **Context stacks on one area:** the area → liquidity built against it → that liquidity swept into the area → a block there. One sweep does two jobs: it adds context and it uses up the pool.
- **There is no fixed spot in an area.** It has tips and a middle; price may turn at any of them. The retrace may stop at the middle (50%) and not give the premium.
- Arrays: order block = last opposite candle engulfed by the very next one, body only, clean or dim; breaker = body of the last candle before the leg; an OTE belongs to its own range and time.

## The trade card (write it before any order; it goes in the overlay as `idea` and the watcher follows it)
| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trade | side, and the higher-timeframe reason (wick or body) |
| Goal | the level the higher timeframe names: "the REAL goal" |
| Context | what · where · when |
| Missing | what must still happen before an entry |
| Entry / stop | the array and its timeframe; the stop at the edge of the area the entry sits in |
| Cancel if | a candle **closes** beyond the next liquidity past the first target ("any form of sellside liquidity under or lower than the first TP1") |
| Missed if | the goal itself is closed through, with more candles beyond, before the fill |
| Money | 80% at the goal; nearer targets are small partials; 20% or less runs |

## Entries he has shown (each needs its context first)
1. **Candle closes → look back → 1m break of structure → return to the gap it left.** Stop beyond the extreme made in the zone.
2. **A flipped gap retested** from the other side (bodies do not close back through).
3. **The middle of an order block,** stop at the block's far edge (body).
4. **From the 5m when the 15m and 1h give no array:** the candle that closes beyond the 50% of the 1h gap and leaves its own 5m gap; enter in that 5m gap, stop at the 1h gap's far edge (NY-1, his own entry; could be placed 09:42).
5. **Created liquidity → displacement the other way → return to its array** (US100 03:30, the first "S" trade).
6. **Inside a missed trade:** equal highs created → an equal low broken → a 5m body closes inside the gap below (a wick would not count) → 1m displacement and break → limit in the 1m gap, stop above the 5m gap. Price may tip the range low first, creating more sellside, then come back and fill (03:58, the second "S" trade).
- Close to the liquidity price is reaching for, the retrace is shallow.
- In a fast market the order goes on at the first return; the write-up comes after.

## Managing it
- **An unfilled order stays on** "until a major form of liq is hit and it feels like a retracement might not follow through". A near target touched and price coming back is not that.
- **A wick is not a break.** Cancel on a closed candle beyond the cancel level; before that "price could still play around a bit".
- **The range that matters** is the swing to the goal, not the small range the entry was found in.
- **Read the retrace:** small equal highs forming under a sell entry = creating, "this area won't get swept soon": the fill may not come; accept the miss and look for the lower entry.
- **Chasing** = "when the bigger TF low is swooped and you're trying to squeeze your way in". After the goal is swept, what is left is runner territory, and "we don't like runners alone": skip it (NY-2, NY-3). Before the goal is swept, a later entry is not a chase.
- **A missed trade is a missed trade.** Being wrong-sided is part of the process.

## Time
- His key windows, New York: 08:20–09:00, 09:20–10:00, 10:20–11:00, 13:20–14:00. Watch times are hard limits ([[hunting-process]]).
- London is watched for learning; New York is favoured for pace. Note when the day's first displacement comes and what it opened.

## Conduct
- Every report leads with conditions, eaten, created, then the trade and its goal. They are fixed lines in the overlay (`now`, `idea`) so the page shows them.
- Draw marks, never text; every mark gets an end where it stopped mattering; keep the chart from getting busy; words go in "Claude's read", kept current.
- Paper trade to learn; log each idea with its card, the conditions, the result and what I learned. An idea taken in chop is labelled so.
- Never save a reading he has not confirmed. When he says I have not found it: look back, look for gaps, look for liquidity at the edges of the marked areas, and say plainly what I do not know.
- After a move, look back at what was there.
