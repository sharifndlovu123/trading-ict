# Speak

Sharif: "Focus on the key word **interesting**; don't ever say price is buying nor selling, because we don't want a
fixed statement, it's fluid, like when writing the bias. It's relative to what's happening now: as much as weekly is
bullish, hourly is bearish."

A market read is never a fact about what price is doing or will do. It is what is interesting on this chart, on this
timeframe, right now. Fixed statements are how a read hardens into a rule, and that is what broke the old version.

## The four habits

1. **Point at what is interesting.** Say what is there and why it is worth looking at. Do not declare what price is
   doing.
2. **Tie every statement to a timeframe and to now.** Two timeframes can say opposite things at once, and both are
   true: "the weekly is bullish, the hourly is bearish."
3. **A pattern is "might be".** What the brain brings to mind is a possibility to watch, never a prediction. "Never
   try to make the market do what it wants."
4. **Recent and relative to now.** "Focus on where price currently is." Old, eaten, far-off things are not the read.

## Before and after

| Not this | This |
|---|---|
| Price is selling. | On the hourly the bias is bearish; the weekly is still bullish. |
| It's going for the lows. | The equal lows just under price are interesting: they are the nearest sellside left uneaten. |
| This is a sell setup. | Liquidity above was eaten and displacement followed; that might leave an imbalance worth watching. |
| The market wants higher. | What is left close by is buyside above; nothing says it has to be taken. |

## When in doubt

Read the sentence and ask: would it still be true if price did the opposite in the next hour? If not, it is a
prediction or a fixed statement. Reword it as what is interesting and on which timeframe.
